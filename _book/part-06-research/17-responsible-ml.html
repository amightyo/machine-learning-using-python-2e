17  Explainable, Fair, and Responsible Machine Learning

17.1 Learning Objectives

  • Distinguish global and local explanations.
  • Apply feature-importance and explanation methods cautiously.
  • Evaluate calibration and subgroup performance.
  • Explain common fairness criteria and their tradeoffs.
  • Conduct a model-risk assessment appropriate to the application.

17.2 Why This Matters

A model can be accurate overall while failing important populations, providing misleading explanations, or being inappropriate for the decision in which it is embedded.

17.3 Real-World Problem

A health model performs well overall but systematically underpredicts risk for one subgroup. Should it be deployed?

17.8 Hands-On Practice

Audit a classifier for calibration, subgroup performance, and explanation stability.

17.9 Common Mistakes

  • Treating SHAP values as causal effects.
  • Reporting only aggregate performance.
  • Assuming removing protected variables removes bias.
  • Choosing a fairness metric without considering context.

17.10 Knowledge Check

  1. How do global and local explanations differ?
  2. What is calibration?
  3. Why can fairness criteria conflict?
  4. Why does removing a protected attribute not guarantee fairness?

17.11 Real-World Case Study

17.11.1 Responsible Prediction in Health or Education

17.12 Professor’s Challenge

The most accurate model has the largest subgroup disparity. What evidence and stakeholder input are needed to decide what to do?

17.13 Research Extension

Develop a fairness, calibration, or explainability study with substantive domain relevance.

17.14 Reproducibility Check

Document group definitions, metrics, thresholds, explanation settings, software versions, and all audit decisions.

17.15 Key Takeaways

  • Accuracy is necessary but not sufficient for responsible ML.
  • Explanations are model-dependent and not automatically causal.
  • Fairness requires explicit normative and technical choices.
