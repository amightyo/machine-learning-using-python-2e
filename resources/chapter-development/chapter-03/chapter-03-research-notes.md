# Chapter 3 Research and Editorial Notes

## Current authoritative documentation reviewed

- SciPy statistics documentation: probability distributions, descriptive statistics, hypothesis tests, resampling, and related statistical tools.
- NumPy linear algebra documentation: dot products, matrix products, norms, decompositions, eigenvalues, and solving linear systems.
- scikit-learn StandardScaler documentation: centering and unit-variance scaling, training-set statistics, and scale sensitivity of selected estimators.

## Editorial principles

1. Mathematics should explain algorithms, not function as a gatekeeping prerequisite.
2. Do not overload Chapter 3 with derivations that belong naturally in later algorithm chapters.
3. Every equation should have an interpretation in words.
4. Prefer executable simulations when they reveal statistical behavior.
5. Preserve the distinction between statistical uncertainty and predictive performance.
6. Explicitly connect scaling to leakage prevention.
7. Avoid claiming all algorithms benefit from standardization.
8. Keep mathematical precision connected to measurement and substantive meaning.
9. Use figures selectively: distribution/spread, sampling variation, and gradient descent provide high-value visual intuition.
10. Later chapters should refer back to this chapter rather than re-teach foundational mathematics from scratch.
